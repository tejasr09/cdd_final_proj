# UX research interview guide

## Status

Prepared for human-led research. No participants have been recruited or interviewed. This guide is not consent or ethics approval; the team must follow applicable course and institutional requirements before use.

## Purpose

Understand how people currently notice, assess, communicate, and respond to operational disruptions and dependencies. This research is exploratory. It does not validate the impact-scoring algorithm or establish that a proposed feature is useful.

## Before the session

- Explain the purpose, expected duration, note-taking method, intended use, storage, and who will see the notes.
- Participation is voluntary. A participant may skip a question, pause, or stop at any time without penalty.
- Ask separately for permission before recording, taking identifiable notes, or using a direct quotation.
- Do not record unless the participant explicitly agrees and the team has an approved, secure storage and deletion plan.
- Prefer role-level descriptions and participant codes over names or identifying details.
- Do not request real student/staff records, confidential schedules, credentials, or sensitive institutional information. Use synthetic examples if an example is needed.
- If consent or required institutional approval is unclear, do not conduct or record the session.

## 30–45 minute session

1. **Introduction (3–5 min):** confirm voluntary participation, consent choices, and participant code.
2. **Recent experience (10–15 min):** ask about a real, non-sensitive example.
3. **Current workflow (10 min):** understand information sources, handoffs, decisions, and workarounds.
4. **Needs and uncertainty (5–10 min):** explore how consequences and assumptions are communicated.
5. **Optional concept reaction (5 min):** only after the open questions, describe the proposed flow neutrally; do not present scores as validated predictions.
6. **Close (2 min):** ask what was missed, restate how notes will be used, and thank the participant.

## Open questions

Use the questions flexibly; do not lead the participant toward the proposed application.

### Recent experience

- Can you describe a recent disruption that affected your work or plans, without naming people or sharing confidential details?
- What first made you aware of it?
- What happened next? Please walk me through the steps.
- Which people, activities, resources, or systems were relevant?
- What information was difficult to find or verify?
- How did you decide what needed attention first?

### Dependencies and coordination

- How do you currently learn that one activity or resource depends on another?
- Where are those relationships recorded, if anywhere?
- How do people resolve disagreements about whether a dependency is current?
- What handoffs or delays are common?
- What do you do when information is incomplete or uncertain?

### Options and outcomes

- What options were considered? What made an option feasible or infeasible?
- How were possible downstream effects checked?
- How did you know whether the response worked?
- What would make a tool or report unhelpful in this situation?

### Neutral concept questions (optional)

After explaining that the proposed analysis is an estimate based on entered relationships:

- What would you need to see to judge whether this explanation is useful?
- Which assumptions would you want to inspect or correct?
- What wording might cause someone to over-trust or misunderstand an estimate?
- What would you expect to happen when you test a hypothetical change?

## Neutral probes

- “What happened next?”
- “Can you give a non-sensitive example?”
- “How did you know?”
- “What made that difficult?”
- “What would you do if that information were unavailable?”

Avoid asking whether the participant “likes” an idea as a substitute for observing a real workflow.

## After the session

- Complete the notes template promptly and separate observations from interpretation.
- Remove identifying details; use participant codes.
- Store notes only in the team-approved location with access limited to the project team.
- Record any withdrawal or deletion request and follow the approved process.
- Do not convert an individual comment into a general finding without corroboration.
